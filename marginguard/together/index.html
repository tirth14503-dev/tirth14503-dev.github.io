MarginGuard and MarginGuard for Sellers · Partner links

How buyers and sellers work together.

When a buyer uses MarginGuard and their supplier uses MarginGuard for Sellers, the two businesses can link. A claim goes straight from the buyer’s books to the seller’s Claims desk, and the answer and the credit note come straight back.

Partner links are coming soon. The link isn’t switched on yet. Until it is, a MarginGuard claim letter can be emailed and pasted into MarginGuard for Sellers.

Follow one claim from start to finish

How it works

One claim, from the overcharge to matching books.

  1. 1

    The buyer’s scan circles an overcharge

    Ridgeway Kitchens checks its invoices with MarginGuard. Halden Mills billed 120 sacks of flour at 19.90 on INV-4471, and Ridgeway’s contract says 18.40 from 1 November: $180.00 over.

  2. 2

    The claim goes across

    The two businesses are linked, so the claim goes over the link instead of by email. It carries the invoice line, the contract price it rests on and the letter, and nothing else.

  3. 3

    It lands on the seller’s Claims desk

    At Halden Mills the claim arrives on the Claims desk, against their own invoice INV-4471, with the rows behind it and a reply-by date. Nobody retypes it from an email.

  4. 4

    The seller checks it against their own records

    Halden’s shipment records show 40 sacks went on 29 October, before the 18.40 price began, and 80 after it. They agree in part, $120.00, and send the reason and both shipment records with the answer.

  5. 5

    A credit note travels back

    Halden issues credit note CN-0317 for $120.00 against INV-4471 and sends it over the link as a standard electronic credit note. Ridgeway’s MarginGuard reads it and checks it like any uploaded document.

  6. 6

    Both books match

    Ridgeway’s finance team confirms the credit note and closes the claim at $120.00, with Halden’s reason on file. Both ledgers now show the same invoice, the same credit note and the same balance.

18.40 was agreed no email chain shipped before the new price the same balance, both sides

What travels

A narrow pipe between two separate products.

Over the link

  • Claims the buyer chooses to send, each with the invoice line and the agreement, receipt or payment it rests on.
  • The seller’s answer: agree, agree in part or don’t agree, with the reason and the records the answer shows.
  • Credit notes, as standard electronic credit notes that the buyer’s MarginGuard reads and checks.
  • Where each claim stands, such as recovered or closed.

Never

  • The files either side uploads. They stay where they were uploaded.
  • The buyer’s other suppliers, stock or other prices.
  • The seller’s other customers, costs, margins or drafts.
  • Passwords, internal notes or anyone’s contact details.
  • Money. A message can’t make a payment or change bank details.

Neither side can look into the other’s records: each one only sends. Everything that arrives goes through the same checks as an upload, and a person decides on each side.

Consent and control

Both businesses agree, and either one can leave.

  1. By invitation only

    An admin on either side creates a one-time invite code and passes it on themself; MarginGuard doesn’t send it. It works once and expires after 7 days. An admin at the other business accepts it.

  2. A safety number, read out and typed

    The accepting admin reads an 8-digit safety number to the other, by phone or any other way. The other admin types it in, and Confirm stays off until it matches.

  3. Unlink at any time

    Either admin can unlink, and it takes effect at once. Claims and documents already exchanged stay in each side’s records, except that a seller who closes their account has everything on their side erased.
